Before you build a SaaS product, answer these questions

Clarify your first customer, core workflow, tenant model and support needs before starting SaaS development.

Krunal Pawar·September 9, 2026·3 min read

Who needs the first version?

Describe a specific customer and the problem they experience today. Understand what they currently use, how often the problem occurs and who decides to adopt a new tool. A focused customer profile makes feature decisions easier and keeps the first release connected to a real workflow.

What is the smallest complete workflow?

A minimum release should still let a customer finish something useful. Define the beginning, the steps and the outcome of that workflow. Remove features that are interesting but not necessary to complete it. Establish acceptance criteria in language both the business and developer can assess.

How will organizations and access work?

Decide whether customers are individuals or organizations, how people are invited and which roles they need. Clarify what happens when a user belongs to more than one organization. Tenant boundaries, onboarding and billing are connected product decisions that are expensive to leave ambiguous.

Who will operate and support it?

Plan the responsibilities for deployment, backups, customer support and changes after launch. Determine what staff need to investigate an issue without unnecessary access to customer data. A SaaS product is an ongoing service, so the first-release plan should include its operation as well as its development.
