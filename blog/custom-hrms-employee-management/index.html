What a useful HRMS should do for your team

Plan an HRMS around employee records, attendance exceptions, leave policies and reviewable payroll inputs.

Krunal Pawar·September 9, 2026·3 min read

Connect the employee record

Employee information becomes more useful when attendance, leave and payroll reference the same person and employment context. Establish which records are authoritative and who can change them. Sensitive information requires careful access rules, and employees may need a different view from managers or payroll administrators.

Write down policy exceptions

A policy that sounds simple can behave differently across shifts, holidays and partial-day leave. Gather examples from actual operations, including exceptions and corrections. Agree on effective dates for policy changes and how historical records should be treated. These decisions should guide development and acceptance checks.

Make payroll inputs reviewable

Attendance events should not become unexplained salary numbers. The team needs to understand how attendance, leave and approved adjustments contribute to a payroll run. Provide a deliberate review step and a way to investigate discrepancies before finalization. The exact workflow should reflect the organization’s agreed policies.

Roll out with the people who use it

Test representative records with HR and payroll staff before relying on the new system. Include overnight shifts, missing punches and changed leave decisions where relevant. A short, controlled parallel review can expose assumptions that ordinary happy-path examples miss.
